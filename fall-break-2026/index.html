The pitch

Why this trip

The Eastern Sierra packs the most scenery-per-mile in California — and early October is the sweet spot: golden aspens, empty trails, cold clear nights, and hot springs that still have road access.

Wake up under Whitney

Alpenglow lights the tallest peak in the Lower 48 straight from your tent door at Tuttle Creek.

Movie-set boulders

Scramble the Alabama Hills — arches, orange granite, and film locations from 400+ Westerns.

Soak at Wild Willy's

End the big day chest-deep in a 100°F geothermal pool, watching the sun drop behind the range.

Zero light pollution

Alabama Hills is an International Dark-Sky area. Campfire, s'mores, and the whole Milky Way overhead.

Friday's finale

Wild Willy's Hot Springs

The capstone to the big north day — not the whole trip, just the perfect way to end one. Rock-lined geothermal pools on the Long Valley flats near Mammoth: a short boardwalk in, the big pool around 100°F, an uninterrupted view of the Sierra crest. Free, first-come, best right at sunset.

~100°F Free ~1.5 hr from camp Bring: swimsuit + sandals + headlamp

Heads up: it's on BLM land with an occasionally rough dirt road (check conditions the day-of), and it's a pack-it-in / pack-it-out site — leave it cleaner than you found it.

Don't-skip

Logistics & permits

A few of these are easy to get wrong and would actually sink the trip. Knock the two ⚠️ items out this week.

⚠️ Confirm a Wednesday gear pickup

SC Outfitters' standard weekend cycle is Thursday night → Monday night, but we leave Wednesday. Reply to Keira and lock in a Wed Oct 7 (before 6:30 PM) pickup, or we drive up with no tent. Book at least a week ahead; gear returns Mon Oct 12. All rentals are final/no-refund.

⚠️ Check fire restrictions before leaving

Fall fire danger can trigger bans. Confirm current Inyo NF / BLM Bishop fire rules the week before — if fires are restricted, even Tuttle Creek's rings may be closed and we'd cook on the stove only.

Two free permits

Alabama Hills camping permit — free on Recreation.gov, required for overnight even though we're at Tuttle Creek (grab it as backup for a dispersed night).

California Campfire Permit — free 2-minute online quiz; covers wood fires and the camp stove.

Water & firewood

No reliable potable water at camp in fall — bring ~7 gallons. Collecting firewood is banned, so buy bundles. Both go on the Wednesday supply run since Lone Pine stores may be shut when we arrive.

The Zipcar math

Gas & insurance are included (gas card in the car). But only ~540 mi is included over 3 days; extra miles are $0.67/mi. Our route is ~600–650 mi, so budget a small overage. Return by 6:30 PM Sat — aim to leave Lone Pine by 11 AM.

Altitude & weather

Camp 5,100 ft; hikes 8,000–10,000+ ft. Hydrate, take Thursday easy, watch for headaches. Early Oct: 60–70°F days, 30–40°F nights (below freezing up high). Check the forecast — first snow is possible.

Signal & safety

Cell service is spotty in the canyons — download offline maps (Google Maps + AllTrails) and text someone the plan. Store all food in the car at night (bear country). Pack out everything.

First-come camping

Tuttle Creek has 83 sites and doesn't take reservations — arriving late Wednesday is fine mid-week in October, but have a Plan B site (Lone Pine Campground) if it's somehow full.
